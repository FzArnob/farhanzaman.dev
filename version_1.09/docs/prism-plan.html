Every block of copy sits on its own translucent panel rather than straight on the canvas. That is the only reason text over a moving 3D scene is readable, and it is why the panels are there at all.

Two colours, and nothing between them

Copy never overlaps

Geometry crossfades — a room should dissolve into the next one. Text does not. Each act owns a slice of its window for its copy and the slices do not touch, so the outgoing block reaches zero opacity before the incoming one leaves it. Below 1.5% it also leaves the layout entirely, so not even an anti-aliased edge can overlap. Verified at every boundary: exactly one act is ever legible.

The trackers

Dots down the centre-right for position — no numbers, because the name of the section belongs to the readout at the centre bottom and printing it twice makes the eye choose. The readout says what you are exploring and how far through that section you are; the rail says where you are in the whole journey.

The particle network is yours

The flat site's particleNetwork.ts, reused as the connective tissue between the canvas and the copy — recoloured to the two brand values, at a fifth of the density, with pointer handling off because the 3D canvas owns that. Its opacity follows the scroll: strongest through Works and Expertise where a network means something, almost gone in the gallery where the artwork has to win.

No mode switch

There is no flat/3D toggle. A browser that cannot run WebGL, or a visitor who has asked their OS for reduced motion, gets a static rendering with the same nine sections, the same face and the same colours — chosen for them, never offered as a choice.

Fast on a slow connection

  • The shell has no three.js in it at all. The overlay, the rail and the readout import from three/liveState.ts and lib/band.ts, neither of which touches three — so the hero copy is on screen before the 3D chunk has been requested.
  • Post-processing is gone. 74 KB gzipped and a full-screen render target, for a bloom that additive sprites already give and a colour fringing the mark's own offset layer already is.
  • The connection is a tier input. navigator.connection reporting 2G, Data Saver or under 1.2 Mbps drops straight to the low tier, which asks for the fewest bytes — no shards, no particle layer, DPR 1.
  • Six thumbnails, not eleven photographs. The gallery only requests what is inside the current window, thumbnails first, full resolution only on approach. Works load the front project and its two neighbours.
  • The typeface is local. Titillium comes from view/static, so there is no font request on the critical path.
  • Nothing is polled. One rAF loop drives the scroll, one drives the copy, and the overlay re-renders only when an act boundary is crossed.

Responsive, and nothing broken by overlap

  • The overlay is a fixed layer, so no block can reflow into another. What they can do is cover each other, and that is what the breakpoints are for.
  • Phone: copy goes full width and sits above the readout; the rail loses its track and becomes a dot column at the very edge; the head drops to the flat site's own mobile size and the text to 16px.
  • Landscape phone: a height query tightens the copy to two clamped lines rather than letting it run off the top.
  • Long fields: clamped with a transparent bottom border rather than padding — overflow:hidden clips at the padding edge, so padding lets the next line paint a readable sliver under the clamp. Every character stays in the DOM; the whole text is in the case study.
  • Measured, not assumed: every act is checked at 1280×800 and 390×780 for exactly one legible block and for nothing colliding with the masthead.
01 — starting point

What the reference does, and where it stops

The clip you sent is a good piece of work with one very good idea in it, repeated eight times. Worth naming precisely, because the interesting question is what it doesn't do.

What we keep

  • The faceted crystal as a content vessel — it reads as "shard of a bigger thing", which suits a portfolio.
  • The void: no floor, no horizon, so scale is free.
  • Copy pinned to the lower-left in real DOM, never baked into the texture.
  • Bloom + drifting debris to give the void depth.

Where it stops

  • No journey. The camera barely moves; the object is swapped. Eight beats, one mechanic, and by the fourth you have seen it.
  • The crystal means nothing. It's a nice shape, not their shape. Nothing about it is derived from the brand.
  • Nothing is navigable. You can scroll it. You can't inspect it, spin it, or query it.
  • Dark only. No light mode, no reduced-motion path, no no-WebGL path.
  • It's a showreel. There is no education, no timeline, no skills matrix, no gallery — the hard content problems never come up.
The move

Keep the vessel, throw away the slideshow. Make the camera travel a single continuous path through a world where every section is a different place, and derive the central object from the FZ monogram so the whole thing is unmistakably yours. Then make it actually usable — spinnable, clickable, readable, and gracefully degradable down to the flat site you already have.

02 — thesis

The logo already told us what to build

Your mark is an FZ monogram drawn twice — a crimson layer offset behind a teal layer. That is not a decorative duplicate. That is a chromatic aberration: white light hitting glass and splitting into components that no longer line up.

Your three designations are Sr. Software Engineer, AI Researcher, Art Enthusiast — one person, three outputs. Your assistant is called SyncBot. Your logo is two colours out of register.

So: the site is a prism. One beam of attention enters, one person splits it, three spectra come out, and the visitor travels along the dispersed beam. Every act of the scroll is a position on that beam. That gives us a principled reason for the teal→crimson gradient, an identity for the hero object, a rationale for the chromatic-aberration post-effect, and a wayfinding system — all from assets that already exist.

Nothing new is invented. #00d3b4 and #fd2155 stay. The monogram stays. The copy stays.

03 — scroll map

One timeline, nine acts, no cuts

The whole page is about 900vh of scroll normalised to t ∈ [0,1]. Every animation in the build is a pure function of t — which means the entire site is scrubbable, deterministic, and impossible to desynchronise.

07 — architecture

Four invariants, and everything else follows

1 · One canvas, forever

A single <Canvas> mounts once in the shell and never unmounts. Acts mount and unmount by scroll proximity (±1 act) inside it. Peak scene cost stays flat however much content you add later.

2 · Scroll is the only clock

Every animation is a pure function of t. No timers, no useState in the render loop, no independent tweens that can drift out of sync. Scrub backwards and the world runs exactly backwards.

3 · The shard pool is global

256 shards in one InstancedMesh, allocated at boot, freed never. Acts lease slices and write matrices. This is what makes the Forge's shatter-and-reassemble handoff possible at all.

4 · The DOM is the content of record

Not a single character of your copy lives in a texture. The canvas is aria-hidden; the text over it is real, selectable, translatable and crawlable. Delete the canvas and you still have a readable site.

08 — budgets

How each ceiling is held

  • Draw calls — the 256 shards are one call; dust is one; slabs, tiles and blades are instanced per act.
  • Zero allocation — pre-allocated Vector3/Matrix4 scratch objects at module scope, and the shard pool leases rather than creating. No garbage means no stutter.
  • Textures — only the active project's media plus its two neighbours are resident. Everything else is disposed on the way out. Remote images are downscaled to 1024² through a canvas before upload, anisotropy 4.
  • LCP — the hero copy is server-shaped DOM that paints before the 3D chunk has even been requested. The prism fades in behind text that is already readable.
  • Idle — frameloop="demand" when the scroll has been still for 400 ms and nothing is animating. A parked tab renders nothing.
  • Off-screen — an IntersectionObserver stops the loop entirely when the canvas is out of view.
09 — accessibility & the flat path

The site has to survive losing the 3D

Keyboard

Tab reaches every act in order. PgUp/PgDn jump act to act. Inside an act, arrows step the interactive element — ring index, lattice node, blade, gallery position. Nothing is hover-only.

Reduced motion

Respected as a hard switch to flat mode, not a slowed-down version of the same ride. Users who set that flag are telling you camera travel makes them ill.

Screen readers

The canvas is aria-hidden="true". Each act is a <section aria-labelledby> with its real heading. A skip link jumps straight to contact.

Touch

Vertical always scrolls the page. Drag-to-spin is horizontal-only. No gesture the browser already owns gets hijacked.

Contrast

All copy sits on DOM surfaces with a token background, never directly on the canvas. That's why #00947f exists in your light.css — the same rule applies here.

SEO

Text volume is unchanged, headings keep their hierarchy, and every project keeps a crawlable anchor. A 3D site that de-indexes you is a bad trade.

10 — risks

What can actually go wrong

RiskSeverityMitigation
Flickr textures blocked by CORShigh The data carries 83 remote image URLs — 8 project logos, 53 media items and 22 gallery images — and 73 of them sit on live.staticflickr.com, the other 10 on assorted third-party hosts. A WebGL texture needs CORS headers, and a third-party CDN can withdraw them without notice. Fix: copy them into view/static/works/ and /gallery/, pre-sized to 1024². Kills the dependency, halves the bytes, and lets us ship 2× WebP. Do this in phase 0 — it's the one task that blocks the Forge and the Gallery.
Transmission cost on mid-range phoneshigh Transmission renders the scene to an offscreen buffer per object. Held by the one-object rule plus the tier fallback to a fresnel fake, which is visually close at a fraction of the cost. Verified on device in phase 7, not assumed.
React 18 vs @react-three/fiber v9medium R3F v9 needs React 19. Pin v8.17 / drei 9.114 and leave the React upgrade as its own separate piece of work — mixing the two would put the 1,858-line SyncBot at risk for no gain.
Bundle weightmedium three + R3F + drei + postprocessing is roughly 230 KB gzipped. Lazy-load the whole 3D chunk after first paint; the flat hero is what LCP measures. Import drei per-symbol, never the barrel.
Scroll hijack fatiguemedium 900vh of scroll is a lot to ask. Mitigated by never blocking or snapping the scroll, by the act rail letting anyone jump, and by deep links flying rather than scrolling. If the analytics in track-visitor.php show drop-off at a specific t, shorten that act — the timeline is data, so this is a one-line change.
Nested scroll on the arcade / gallerylow Any inner scroller inside a scroll-driven page is a trap. The arcade uses a horizontal cylindrical grid, and the gallery uses camera position, so there is no second vertical scroller anywhere in the build.
Content driftlow The JSON schema does not change, so admin/ and ProfileStore keep working. New expertises and projects appear in the Lattice and the Forge automatically — the ring divides 360° by projects.length, and the lattice re-solves its node positions.
Safari transmission artefactslow Known ANGLE issues with high-samples transmission on iOS. Clamp samples to 6 on Safari and prefer the fresnel fake below iOS 17.

bugs the plan could not have predicted — found by running it
SymptomCause
Nothing drew at allreact-three-fiber hands rendering to the app if any useFrame uses a positive priority. Priorities 1 and 2, used purely to order shard leases, silently disabled the render loop. Negative priorities sort identically and leave it alone.
Scroll snapped back mid-pageThe rig's context object was rebuilt on every act change, so the deep-link effect re-ran and re-seeked. Splitting the stable API from the changing act fixed it — and stopped the whole overlay re-rendering eight times a page.
Wrong act on screen on a slow deviceThe damped scroll follower clamped dt to 50 ms. An exponential follower needs the real elapsed time; at 2 fps the clamp closed only a third of the gap per frame and t trailed the scroll by seconds.
The rail named one project, the camera showed anotherThe ring rotated the wrong way. Rotating by θ moves a core from angle a to a−θ, so bringing core i forward needs +a. Exactly the desync the one-timeline invariant exists to prevent, and invisible until DOM and 3D were checked against each other.
Every lattice node rendered blackvertexColors: true on an instanced mesh switches on USE_COLOR, which multiplies by a per-vertex color attribute the geometry has not got. instanceColor alone is what three needs.
Copy could not be selected; jumps driftedThe existing 01-base.css sets user-select: none and scroll-behavior: smooth on *. The first contradicts "the DOM is the content of record"; the second double-smooths against Lenis. Both are corrected under a root class, so the flat site is untouched.
